From Our Community

In their own words

These stories come from participants, graduates, volunteers, and families. Each one is different. Each one is true.

Graduate • Houston
Melvin Workman
“I stopped thinking like a prisoner and started thinking like a CEO.”

Melvin entered the program thinking he knew what entrepreneurship looked like. He’d hustled his whole life—just never legally. PEP didn’t just teach him a business plan. It rewired how he saw himself. Today he runs a logistics company employing three other PEP alumni and speaks regularly at graduation ceremonies about what it means to lead with character.

Graduate • North Texas
Steven Gutterud
From Self-Centered to Servant Leader

Steven had spent decades focused on himself—on survival, on advantage, on staying ahead. When he walked into a PEP classroom and was asked to serve someone else first, something shifted. “That was the moment,” he says. “That was the classroom, right there.” Since his release, Steven has mentored dozens of men still inside and leads a small construction firm that hires returning citizens exclusively.

Read Full Story
Graduate • Business Owner • Houston
Emmanuel Akingbola
“PEP didn’t give me a second chance. It gave me a first one.”

Emmanuel grew up watching people around him fall into cycles he thought were inevitable. By his twenties, he was part of one. After years in and out of the system, he found PEP inside a Texas state prison—and for the first time, someone handed him tools instead of consequences. He earned his business certificate. He wrote a plan. He pitched it in front of executives who had never set foot in a prison. And when he walked out, he didn’t walk back in. Today, Emmanuel runs a cleaning and facilities company with twelve employees. Four of them are PEP graduates. “I know what they’re capable of,” he says simply. “I was one of them.”
